What changed in this revision

The previous variant table (V0…V5) moved three area lights to per-variant absolute world coordinates tuned against one scene's geometry. Two of those coordinate sets landed outside the room and rendered at essentially zero radiance — the "near-zero radiance" caveat this report used to carry. Nothing about that failure was detectable from the recipe itself, and it would recur on any new scene.

The replacement, hssd_blender_ir/lighting.py, is position-robust by construction and driven by the scene's own contents:

  • Sun — a Blender SUN, which has a direction (elevation/azimuth) and no position, so it cannot fall outside a room.
  • Sky — a world-background node; no position at all.
  • Windows — inward-facing area lights spanning the scene's own opening assemblies (thin-axis detection, oriented toward the interior centroid, horizontal apertures skipped).
  • Fixtures — point lights placed inside the scene's own luminaires, found via the HSSD semantic taxonomy (super_category == "lighting"), at a category-dependent height fraction of each asset's bounding box. This scene has 4 pendant ceiling_lamps at z≈2.14 m and 1 table_lamp at z≈1.10 m. Name matching alone was rejected: it wrongly catches assets like "AESOP Ginger Flight Therapy".

Time of day drives sun, sky and windows together; fixtures layer on/off and colour-temperature diversity on top, deterministically seeded on (variant, fixture index) so a re-render reproduces the same room, with at least one lamp always lit when a variant asks for any.

Why the window layer is load-bearing: these rooms are closed shells and the opaque-glass substitution deliberately removes the transmissive panes, so sun and sky alone reach the interior with zero radiance — the first render of T0/T1/T2 came out fully black (max = 0.000 / 0.620 / 0.055). The portals are therefore the only daylight path, which also means their power alone sets the interior exposure; window.watts_per_m2 is a calibrated number, not a physical one.

Roughly a 20:1 midday-to-night ladder. The first calibration pass ran the daylight portals at 900/420/320 W/m², which clipped T0 and T1 to white (mean 20.2 and 9.4 against a well-exposed T3 at 1.27); they are now 135/80/68.

All 6 variants share the same camera, geometry, glass substitution, and PBR/geometry GT for a given pose — only illumination changes. RGB uses a monotonic HDR tone curve (v/(1+v), γ 2.2), so the night frames are shown at true relative brightness rather than per-frame auto-exposed.
